Data sources & licenses

The default sources need no paid account or API key for personal, noncommercial use. Detail varies by location and survey date. A finer mesh or a higher tile zoom cannot add features missing from the original survey or photograph.

DataPreferred sourceCoverage & use
ElevationUSGS 3DEP best availableUS coverage, including lidar-derived detail where available. Public domain. Local height samples are requested at approximately 1 m; High terrain meshes remain approximately 5 m.
Aerial imageryUSGS NAIP Plus / high-resolution orthoimageryConterminous US. Public-domain NAIP and contributed orthophotography; the service describes source resolutions from 6 inches to 1 m, depending on location.
Aerial fallbackGeoPlatform NAIP and USGS NAIPConterminous US. Public-domain USDA imagery, typically 0.3–1 m. USGS's cached imagery provides the initial overview. Fine detail uses aerial exports, rather than enlarged overview tiles.
Global imageryEOxCloudlessSentinel-2 mosaic, about 10 m. CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, free for personal and other noncommercial uses with attribution and ShareAlike conditions. EOX terms.
Topo & namesUSGS The National Map / GNISUS topographic maps and geographic names. No automatic Esri or OpenTopoMap fallback.
Elevation fallbackMapzen Terrain Tiles on AWSGlobal coverage assembled from regional sources. Native resolution and license vary; provider credits follow below. A packaged USGS 3DEP Enchantments survey is also available.

Slow or missing data retains the available terrain and imagery. Fallback elevation retries the preferred source while remaining visible. PNG exports add no further JPEG compression; elevation is transferred as floating-point heights without compression error. These export formats do not recover detail already lost in the original data.

There is no single globally finest free source. Local public surveys can exceed these general services, and high-resolution coverage is not universal. The app does not claim that every location has 1 m elevation or sub-metre imagery.

Optional providers

Esri World Imagery and Bing via Cesium Ion are available only through explicit configuration. Their account, usage, and imagery rights have separate terms. The default configuration does not select them, even when an optional token exists, and never falls back to them.
